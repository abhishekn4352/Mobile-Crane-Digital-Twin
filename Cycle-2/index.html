01 / Machine

What is a Mobile Crane?

A mobile crane is a cable-controlled or hydraulic-powered crane mounted on rubber-tired carriers or crawler tracks, integrating its own drive system. It is designed to travel to and position itself at a work site without requiring disassembly.

Mobile cranes combine the lifting capability of fixed cranes with rapid on-site mobility, making them essential in construction, infrastructure, energy, and industrial operations worldwide.

Their defining characteristic is self-propelled integration — the crane and carrier are a single unit capable of road travel and on-site lifting with hydraulic outrigger stabilization.

05 / Mechanics

The Operating Sequence.

From site arrival to lift completion — six phases of mobile crane operation, each with its own mechanical and safety requirements.

01 — Positioning

Site Positioning

The crane drives to the designated lift area under its own power. The operator selects the optimal position relative to the load and placement point, accounting for ground conditions, overhead clearances and working radius requirements.

02 — Stabilization

Outrigger Deployment

Outrigger beams extend laterally and hydraulic cylinders lower the outrigger pads onto prepared ground. This transfers the crane's weight from the tires to the pads, significantly increasing the rated capacity and operating stability.

03 — Boom Position

Boom Luffing

The boom lift cylinder raises or lowers the main boom to the required angle. The boom angle sensor monitors inclination continuously. The operator references the load chart to confirm safe operating conditions at the selected angle.

04 — Telescoping

Boom Extension

Internal hydraulic cylinders extend the telescopic boom sections to the required working radius. A boom length sensor tracks extension. The load capacity rating changes with boom length — longer reach means reduced rated load.

05 — Load Handling

Lift Execution

The load line is lowered via the hoist winch, the hook block is rigged to the load, and lifting begins. The load moment indicator continuously monitors the combination of load weight and working radius against the rated load chart.

06 — Monitoring

System Monitoring

Throughout the operation, sensors report boom angle, boom length, load, hydraulic pressure, and outrigger status to the monitoring system. The system manages the safe working envelope and alerts the operator to approaching limits.

08 / Safety

Load Management &
Safety Systems.

Mobile crane safety is governed by a hierarchy of monitoring, limiting, and operator-awareness systems working simultaneously during every lift.

Load Moment Indicator

Continuously calculates the product of load weight and working radius. Warns the operator as the rated load moment is approached and can trigger protective functions at defined limits.

Anti-Two-Block Device

A limit switch at the boom tip detects when the hook block approaches the boom head. Activates an automatic cutout to prevent the wire rope from over-tensioning and failing catastrophically.

Outrigger Deployment

Sensors confirm full outrigger extension and pad contact before full rated capacity is available. Operating with partial outrigger deployment significantly reduces rated load and stability margins.

Boom Angle & Length Limits

Minimum and maximum boom angle limits prevent structural overload and tipping. Extension limits prevent unsafe configurations. All limits are programmed from the manufacturer's rated load chart.

Wind & Environmental Monitoring

Anemometers measure wind speed on larger cranes. Operating limits are specified for wind conditions, as wind-induced dynamic loads on the boom and suspended load are critical safety factors.

Operator Awareness Systems

Modern cranes provide a comprehensive display of machine status, load chart compliance, rated capacity and proximity to operating limits through the crane's integrated monitoring and display system.

Industry Trends

01

Electrification of Crane Powertrains

Hybrid and fully electric mobile crane variants are entering the market, targeting emissions regulations and operational cost reduction in urban environments.

02

Digital Load Management Systems

Advanced LMIs with real-time load chart processing, automatic working envelope management, and operator-assist functions are becoming standard.

03

Remote Monitoring & Telematics

Fleet management platforms connect cranes to central monitoring systems for utilization data, maintenance scheduling, and operational analytics.

04

Stricter Global Safety Regulations

Evolving standards in major markets are increasing requirements for automatic safety systems, operator certification, and machine inspection intervals.
